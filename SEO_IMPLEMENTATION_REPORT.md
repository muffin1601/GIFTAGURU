# SEO Implementation Report — Gifta Guru

Date: 2026-09-05
Scope: full storefront SEO + content architecture, implemented in the application.

---

# Executive Summary

The storefront had a genuinely good technical SEO foundation already — a single
`pageMetadata()` helper enforcing canonical/OG/Twitter on every page, a
database-driven sitemap, a correctly reasoned `robots.txt`, and JSON-LD builders
that deliberately refuse to emit fake ratings. That foundation was kept and built
on, not replaced.

What it did not have was **content**. Product pages carried a one-sentence
description; category pages carried a tagline; the homepage had almost no
crawlable prose and no metadata export of its own. There were no industry,
use-case, seasonal, multi-piece or guide pages at all, so roughly two-thirds of
the keyword dataset had nowhere to land.

This implementation added that content tier and wired the keyword architecture
into code so it can be verified rather than asserted:

- **731 unique keywords** mapped, each with a documented disposition, in `SEO_KEYWORD_MAP.csv`
- **38 new pages** (33 landing pages + 5 hubs), each with unique copy, FAQs and internal links
- **24 product pages** rewritten with distinct primary keywords, features, use cases and FAQs
- **4 category pages** given cluster-head-term ownership, buying guidance and FAQs
- **Homepage** given its own metadata, an indexable intro, and a 16-link routing block
- **1 real cannibalization conflict** in the source strategy found and resolved
- **2 automated scripts + 6 regression tests** so the architecture cannot silently rot

Build, typecheck, lint and all 41 tests pass. No pricing, inventory, payment,
auth or admin behaviour was touched.

## Two corrections to the brief's premises

Both were verified in the data, and both change what "cover every keyword" means.

**1. The dataset is 731 keywords, not 891.** Every one of the 160 rows on the
"3-6 Piece Keywords" sheet also appears verbatim on the "SEO Keywords" sheet —
verified by exact string match. That sheet is a filtered *view* of the
multi-piece subset, not an additional 160 terms. The mapping de-duplicates and
records which sheet(s) each keyword came from.

**2. Roughly 21% of the dataset should not be targeted at all.** 154 keywords are
excluded on quality or integrity grounds, detailed below. Targeting them would
have required either publishing pages nobody searches for, or publishing
specifications for products the catalog does not sell.

# Current SEO Architecture

```
Homepage                      broad brand head terms
   |
   +-- Category pages (4)     cluster head terms
   |      |
   |      +-- Product pages (24)   one long-tail term each
   |
   +-- Industry landing (9)   sector-specific commercial terms
   +-- Use-case landing (7)   occasion / programme terms
   +-- Occasion landing (5)   seasonal terms, evergreen URLs
   +-- Multi-piece sets (4)   piece-count terms
   +-- Guides (8)             informational terms
```

The rule that holds it together: **product pages never target cluster head
terms**. They link upward instead, via `lib/seo/content/clusters.ts`, which maps
each of the seven product clusters to the category and use-case pages that own
those terms. This is enforced by a test, not by convention.

# Product Strategy

All 24 products now carry, via `lib/seo/content/products.ts`:

- a **unique** primary keyword (asserted by test)
- 5–8 secondary keywords worked naturally into the copy
- hand-written SEO title and meta description
- 3 paragraphs of detailed description, 6 key features, 4 use cases
- 2 FAQs each (48 total), emitted as FAQPage schema
- 3 cluster links passing relevance upward

Average product copy: ~248 words, up from a single sentence.

**One deliberate content change:** the Content Pack's copy stated hard minimums
("minimum 25 pieces", "50-piece minimum", "10-piece minimum"). The application's
actual `MIN_ORDER_QUANTITY` is **5**, store-wide and admin-configurable via
`/admin/settings`. Publishing the pack's numbers would have put false ordering
policy on 24 live product pages. All hard piece-counts were removed and replaced
with relative phrasing ("low minimums", "built for volume"), which preserves the
commercial message without contradicting the store.

# Industry Strategy

Nine pages, at `/industries/[slug]`: IT & SaaS, fintech, BFSI, healthcare &
pharma, manufacturing, real estate, consulting, ecommerce & retail, education &
edtech.

The dataset carries industry tags for ~28 sectors, but most hold only 6–10
keywords that restate each other ("telecom joining kit", "telecom onboarding
kit", "telecom employee gifts"). A tag earned a page only where it had: enough
distinct keyword variation, a gifting motion genuinely specific to the sector,
products in this catalog that suit it, and something useful to say that the
category page does not already say.

The remaining ~19 sectors are covered in a "Sectors covered without a dedicated
page" section on the `/industries` hub, which routes each to the use-case page
where its intent actually resolves. 66 keywords map there. **This is the single
most important restraint in the whole implementation** — building a page per
industry tag would have produced two dozen interchangeable pages differing by one
noun, which is doorway-page behaviour and a ranking risk rather than a gain.

# Seasonal Strategy

Five evergreen pages at `/occasions/[slug]`: Diwali, New Year, festive, wedding
season, work anniversaries and milestones.

Deliberately **evergreen URLs, not campaign pages**. No page names a year or a
date, so none goes stale and none needs an annual rebuild — seasonal demand
climbs well before the occasion, and a URL accumulating authority across years
outperforms one published each autumn. Lead-time guidance is expressed relatively
("several weeks ahead", "before the rush") rather than as a promised dispatch
date, because real timelines are a store setting the team controls.

A 12-month refresh calendar is in `SEO_CONTENT_ROADMAP.md`. The two windows that
matter: **Diwali planning starts in August**, and **new-year planner gifting must
be ordered in November**.

# Blog / Content Strategy

Eight guides at `/guides/[slug]`, averaging ~430 words with 3 FAQs each:

corporate-gifting-guide (pillar), employee-welcome-kit-guide, client-gifting-guide,
corporate-gifting-budget-guide, eco-friendly-corporate-gifting,
diwali-corporate-gifting-guide, conference-giveaway-guide,

Editorial standard applied: every guide contains advice a buyer could act on even
if they never bought anything here. No invented statistics, no cited research
that does not exist, no prices (the catalog owns those and they change). Where a
guide recommends a set, it says why — including where it recommends *against*
spending more (the 6-piece page explicitly says six mediocre items beat by four
good ones).

# Image SEO

Audited; **largely unchanged, and that is the correct outcome.** The existing
implementation already uses `next/image` throughout with explicit `sizes`,
`fill`, lazy loading by default and `priority` on above-the-fold images, and
product `alt` text comes from the database (`image.alt ?? product.name`) so
admins control it per image. The category hero image correctly uses `alt=""`
since it is decorative and the H1 carries the meaning.

The one genuine gap is **filenames**: catalog images are `gift_set (1).png`,
`corporate_gift_set_300x300_v2.png` and similar. These are admin-uploaded assets
in Supabase storage, not repository files — renaming them is a data migration
that would break every existing `imageUrl` row, so it is listed under
Recommended Next Steps rather than done unilaterally.

# Cannibalization Audit

**One real conflict found in the source strategy, and resolved.**

The Content Pack assigned `eco friendly corporate gifts` as *both* the Eco
category head term *and* the primary keyword of the Sage Green Sustainable Gift
Set. Two pages cannot own one primary.

Resolution: the **category page** is the canonical owner — it is a browse-intent
query best served by a listing of every eco set — and the Sage Green product
moved to `sustainable gift set for employees`, previously its strongest
secondary. Its title and meta description were rewritten to match.

Two further conflicts were **prevented** during the build rather than shipped:
- `custom stationery gift set` was on the pack's Premium-category target list *and*
  is the Complete Stationery Gift Set's primary. Dropped from the category.
- `corporate gifts for clients` would have collided between the Client
  Appreciation Desk Set and the client use-case page. The product keeps `client
  appreciation gifts`; the broader browse term went to the use-case page.

The validator additionally caught **11 cases of a page listing another page's
primary as its own secondary** — a subtler form of the same problem. All 11 were
rewritten to non-conflicting terms.

Current state: **0 cannibalization conflicts** across 61 keyword-owning pages,
enforced by two regression tests.

# Keywords Not Used and Why

154 keywords (21%), in two groups:

### Machine-generated label concatenations — 144 keywords

The dataset contains rows like `Coaching / EdTech joining kit`, `BFSI / Insurance
luxury corporate gifts`, `Renewable Energy / ESG premium corporate gifts` — the
industry column's label pasted onto a generic term, **including the spreadsheet's
own " / " separator**. Nobody types these into a search engine.

Targeting them would mean publishing copy built around a phrase with no demand.
They are excluded with that reason recorded per keyword. Note this does not
abandon the underlying *intent*: "Telecom joining kit" is junk as a string, but
telecom onboarding gifting is covered on the `/industries` hub and
`/gifting/employee-onboarding`.

### Product types not in the catalog — 10 keywords

`premium wireless charging corporate gift set`, `premium gadget gift set for
corporate employees`, `executive tech accessories gift box`, `tech employee
welcome kit with laptop accessories`, `premium desk setup gift box India`,
`bamboo corporate gifts`, `bamboo corporate gift bundle`, `organic cotton
corporate gifting kit`, `seed paper employee welcome kit`, `curated employee
wellness gift box`.

The catalog is stationery, pens, journals, folios and planners. Ranking for these
would require publishing specifications for goods that do not exist here — which
is both an SEO liability (immediate bounce) and dishonest. Flagged for revisit if
the range expands; the mapping records them individually so they surface the day
new products are added.

# Remaining Risks

1. **Image filenames** remain non-descriptive. A data migration, not a code change — see next steps.
2. **The 24 product pages depend on slug stability.** `lib/seo/content/products.ts` is keyed by slug; if an admin changes a product slug, that product silently loses its SEO copy. The validator catches this (`seo-content-for-missing-product`) but only when run.
3. **Products added by admins get no editorial copy** until an entry is written. They degrade to catalog-derived metadata rather than breaking — deliberate, but it means the validator's `product-without-seo-content` warning should be watched.
4. **No `hreflang`.** Correct for now — the site is single-locale (`en_IN`).
5. **Faceted navigation / filter URLs** on `/shop` were not audited in depth; if filter combinations become crawlable URLs later they will need canonical or noindex handling.
6. **Content Pack minimums were removed** as false. If the business genuinely does enforce per-product minimums, they should be added to the database as product data and rendered from there — not reinstated in editorial copy.
